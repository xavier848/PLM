# 2.2.2 Lifecycle Assessment

Course: PLM 500 Product Lifecycle Management I, Munich Business School. Topic 2: Product Life Cycle Management & Sustainability, Required Learning Materials. Original page: https://lms.munich-business-school.de/mod/page/view.php?id=72743

**Life cycle assessment**  (LCA) - an LCA identifies *environmental*  impacts of products, processes, or services (also referred to as an environmental life cycle assessment, eLCA, E-LCA, or ELCA). LCAs are used (1) to evaluate the impact of a current product, process, or service, (2) in the product design and development stage identify the most environmentally-friendly materials, (3) during the product re-design stage to improve products, (4) for strategic planning, (5) to identify the best policy option, and (6) for marketing and communication about the environmental impacts of products, processes, or services. An LCA has 4 steps: goal and scope definition, inventory analysis, impact assessment, and interpretation. LCAs are performed using Umberto, SimaPro, GaBi, or open-source OpenLCA software. With some extra work to find public databases that contain the necessary data on raw materials, an LCA can also be calculated manually or in a spreadsheet. This is a valuable skill to acquire to work in the field of sustainability.

In the first step of goal and scope definition, companies choose the extent to which they want to measure the environmental impact. The scope of an LCA can be

- **Cradle-to-gate**  - this is the simplest method but also the least comprehensive, it only covers the environmental impact from the acquisition of raw materials to the point of finished production.
- **Cradle-to-customer** - is the next level of LCA that includes impact along the value chain to the point of the customer.
- **Cradle-to-grave**  - is the next level of LCA that includes impact to the point of product disposal.
- **Cradle-to-cradle** - this is the most comprehensive LCA that considers environmental impact from raw material extraction, to production, distribution, end use, end-of-life, and disposal method (discard, recycle, reuse, etc.). This is the only "complete" LCA, all the others are considered "partial" LCAs.

Five life cycle stages are arranged in a clockwise circle with black icons and arrows: Extraction (a mining dump truck, top), Production (a factory, upper right, circled in red with the red label 'Gate to gate'), Distribution (a delivery truck, lower right), Use (a person icon, lower left), End of life (a waste bin, left). Four colored outer arrows around the circle show different system boundaries for a life cycle assessment (LCA), from narrowest to widest: 'Cradle to gate' (gray, innermost, covers Extraction to Production); 'Cradle to customer' (blue, extends through Distribution to the customer); 'Cradle to grave' (yellow/orange, covers the whole cycle through Use up to End of life); 'Cradle to cradle' (green, outermost, a full loop that closes the cycle, i.e. includes recycling/reuse back into new production). The red circle 'Gate to gate' covers only the Production stage. Meaning: the LCA scope (system boundary) defines which stages are included in the assessment: gate to gate = production only; cradle to gate = extraction and production; cradle to customer = up to delivery to the customer; cradle to grave = entire life including use and disposal; cradle to cradle = full closed loop with recycling.
