# 1.3 Social Sustainability

Course: PLM 500 Product Lifecycle Management I, Munich Business School. Topic 1: Sustainability Introduction, Required Learning Materials. Original page: https://lms.munich-business-school.de/mod/page/view.php?id=70775

If environmental sustainability can be thought of as protecting our life supporting systems, then social sustainability can be thought of as preserving and enhancing our quality of life systems.

The Brundtland Report (1987) considered social sustainability to include meeting basic needs and “extending to all the opportunity to satisfy their aspirations for a better life." The report concludes that we must ensure *equitable opportunities*  for all. The key words here that define social sustainability are EQUITY and OPPORTUNITY which will ensure quality of life. Equity and opportunity should be considered basic human rights. Lack of equity and opportunity (as well as violation of other human rights) underly many social movements, such as LGBTQ+ rights, women's rights, Black Lives Matter, and the push for Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI).

The “Human Development approach” (Haq, 1995; Sen, 1999) is one way in which we can pursue equity and opportunity for all. This approach differs from historical sustainable development approaches that assume economic growth will improve human wellbeing. In Human Development, the emphasis is on the development of people, not the economy. If we invest in people and provide opportunity, individuals can choose what they value and exercise more control over their livelihoods.

Following this logic, social sustainability, or quality of life, consists of:

- **opportunity**  for all
- **choice**  for individuals to live life on their own terms, by their own values, and to choose which opportunities to take
- **removal of barriers**  that prevent opportunity and choice

Inequality exists in various forms, including gender, ability, race, age, sexual orientation, and more. Persistent lack of opportunity, lack of choice, and systemic barriers underlie many social movements, such as LGBTQ+ rights, women's rights, Black Lives Matter, and the push for Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI).
