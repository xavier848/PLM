# 1.4 Economic Sustainability

Course: PLM 500 Product Lifecycle Management I, Munich Business School. Topic 1: Sustainability Introduction, Required Learning Materials. Original page: https://lms.munich-business-school.de/mod/page/view.php?id=70776

While environmental sustainability refers to life supporting systems and social sustainability refers to quality of life systems, economic sustainability refers to systems for the production and distribution of wealth and other resources in a manner that is fair and just without negatively impacting social and environmental systems.

Consider this image to understand the *just distribution of resources* .

**[Figure: equality equity justice]** Description of the figure: Three-panel illustration titled by captions EQUALITY, EQUITY, JUSTICE. In each panel a tall man (dark blue shirt), a woman (cream shirt) and a small boy (grey shirt) stand at a wooden fence trying to watch a children's soccer game on a field behind it.
- EQUALITY: "Everyone receives the same support, regardless of individual circumstances." Each of the three stands on an identical wooden box, but the boy is still too short to see over the fence (the adults can see anyway).
- EQUITY: "Support is adjusted based on individual needs so everyone has a fair opportunity to participate." The man needs no box, the woman stands on one box, and the boy stands on two stacked boxes, so all three can see over the fence.
- JUSTICE: "The systematic barrier has been removed so everyone can participate equally." The fence is gone; all three stand on the grass with a clear view of the game and no boxes are needed.
Message: equality gives the same help to all, equity gives different help according to need, and justice removes the underlying barrier.

Image source: City for All Women Initiative (2015); Froehle (2016)

It is not uncommon for companies to confuse economic sustainability for financial profitability, but they are not the same thing. While a company must remain financially viable to provide a product or service, economic sustainability asks how society and the planet “profit” from the company's existence. While some refer to the elements of sustainability as people (social sustainability), planet (environmental sustainability), and profit (economic sustainability), we prefer people, planet, and *prosperity* . Economic sustainability considers how the company's activities impact social (quality of life) and environmental (life supporting) systems in pursuit of just production and distribution of resources so we all prosper.

*“Profit is like oxygen. You need it to survive, but if you think that oxygen is the purpose of your life then you're missing something” - Peter Drucker, Management Consultant, Educator, & Author*
